# Institutional brief — Baylor Children's Clinical Centre of Excellence

**Modules:** Chronicle (chronic blood disorder registry) + Transfuse (transfusion workflow)
**Priority:** Beachhead candidate — narrow paediatric scope only
**Contact link:** https://www.bloodchain.life

---

## The problem we are addressing together

Children with sickle cell disease and other chronic blood disorders depend on **scheduled, regular transfusions**. When records live across paper files and separate spreadsheets, scheduled transfusions get missed, care gaps go unnoticed until a crisis, and no one has a single view of a child's transfusion history.

## What Chronicle + Transfuse do

| Capability | Module | Outcome for Baylor |
|------------|--------|--------------------|
| Patient registry for chronic blood disorders | Chronicle | One record per child, longitudinal |
| Scheduled-transfusion tracking + care-gap alerts | Chronicle | Flag a missed or upcoming transfusion before it becomes a crisis |
| Transfusion request and crossmatch workflow | Transfuse | Structured ordering, fewer manual steps |
| Haemovigilance / reaction recording | Transfuse | Documented adverse-event trail |

We pitch this as **a care-coordination tool for the Baylor paediatric transfusion programme** — explicitly not a national registry.

## Pilot proposal

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Scope | ~50 paediatric patients on a chronic transfusion schedule |
| Duration | 90 days |
| Success metric | Care-gap alerts surfaced; reduction in missed scheduled transfusions |
| Pricing | Implementation + licence (funder-backed); figure set after scoping |
| Our commitment | Setup, clinician training, weekly check-in during the pilot |

## What we need from Baylor

1. A clinical coordinator to own the pilot.
2. Confirmation of how transfusion records are kept today (paper / spreadsheet / EMR).
3. Ethics/data-handling guidance for paediatric records.
4. A named clinical champion who can vouch for the tool's usefulness.

## The ask

A scoping call to confirm the records are paper/spreadsheet-based today, then a tightly bounded 50-patient, 90-day pilot.

> **Discovery question to resolve first:** Are Baylor's transfusion records actually paper/spreadsheet-based, or already in an EMR? (See `assumptions-log.md`, Q2.) A named clinical champion here also strengthens every other Bloodchain pitch.
